## Блок 3. США — 1:45–3:50

**Кадры:** карта США крупно → бейдж и пропуск на столе → пустой стол → дверь с
коробкой → ведущий. Одна фишка WorkChip поверх кадра с дверью: **60 DAYS /

> Start with the United States, because it's the outlier, and most people —
> including a lot of Americans — don't realise how far the outlier goes.
>
> The default rule is called at-will employment. Either side can end the
> relationship at any time, for any reason, or for no reason at all. No notice
> period. No written explanation. No process. ✅
>
> There are limits, and they matter: you can't fire someone *because* of race,
> sex, age, disability, religion, or for reporting a crime. But notice the shape
> of that. The law doesn't say the employer needs a good reason. It says there
> are a handful of specific bad reasons. Everything outside that list is fair game.
>
> The one federal rule that does force notice is the WARN Act, and it only bites
> at scale: sixty days' notice, and only for mass layoffs and plant closings. ✅
> One person on a Tuesday? Nothing.

`[INSIDE: почему компании в США всё равно дают две недели и выходное пособие,
хотя закон не требует — репутация на рынке, референсы, риск иска, и то, что
рекрутеры кандидатов из этой компании потом спрашивают «как у вас расстаются».]`

`[INSIDE — нужен твой пример: случай, когда компания дала больше, чем была

> Now the same person, same company, London office.
>
> The Employment Rights Act sets a floor that rises with service. Under two
> years, one week's notice. From two to twelve years, one week for every
> completed year. Twelve years or more, twelve weeks, and that's the ceiling. ✅
>
> But notice isn't the interesting part. The interesting part is the second
> year. Cross two years of continuous service and you get the right not to be
> unfairly dismissed — which means the employer now needs a reason the law
> recognises, and a process it can defend. ✅
>
> Think about what that does to the calendar. In the US, month eighteen and
> month sixty look the same. In the UK they are two different legal universes,
> and the border between them is a date.

`[INSIDE: как двухлетний порог меняет разговор внутри компании — что
происходит с сотрудником на 20-м месяце и почему в командах об этой дате знают

**Кадры:** документ-уведомление → ведущий (поза «говорит») → «The End».

> If you're reading an offer right now, three things are worth finding before
> you sign, and none of them are the salary.
>
> One: what notice does the contract give you, and is it more than the statutory
> floor. Two: what happens during probation, because in most places the floor
> barely applies there. Three: whether anything you're being paid — a bonus,
> equity, a relocation cost — claws back if you leave, or if they let you go.
>
> That's the part people skip. The salary number is the part you negotiate.
> The exit terms are the part that decides what that number was actually worth.
